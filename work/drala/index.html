The story

Drala Mountain Center is a retreat center on 600 acres of mountain valley near Red Feather Lakes, Colorado, and home to the Great Stupa of Dharmakaya. It has more than 100 offerings through the year in meditation, yoga, mindful living and the contemplative arts. I joined in March 2022 as Project Manager & Social Media Coordinator and stayed until January 2025.

Most days I wore two hats. Behind the scenes, I kept retreats, programs and projects on track and looked after the team doing the work. Online, I shared what made the place special with the people who might want to visit. Those three years were a busy, fast-growing time for the center.

A retreat, from both sides. Every program had two halves for me. Before guests arrived, I helped plan capacity and made sure the team knew what to do, using the SOPs and training guides I wrote. While it ran, I looked after the team and the guests, and when something went wrong for someone, I worked on making it right. At the same time, I was making the photos, videos and graphics that showed people what being there felt like. Running programs and sharing them fed each other, and together they helped program registrations grow 35%.

The posts and links below come from the center’s own website and social pages. They show the place and its programs while I was there, not specific pieces I made.

What I did

  • Planned and ran 25+ projects, retreats and wellness programs a year, and delivered 95% of them on time.
  • Led a team of 16 who kept things running for 300+ guests a day.
  • Wrote SOPs and training guides and trained 100+ employees, which cut new-hire onboarding time by 20%.
  • When something went wrong for a guest, I worked on making it right. Guest satisfaction rose 12%.
  • Helped plan capacity and run programs during a stretch when the center’s revenue grew 360% (2022 to 2025).
  • Ran the center’s social media and made 120+ photos, videos and graphics a year. Engagement rose 150%, followers 75% and organic reach 180%, and program registrations grew 35%.

06

Reflection

At Drala I wore two hats, and over time they became one job. Because I helped run the retreats every day, I knew which moments were worth sharing online. And because I was the one sharing them, I learned to see the programs the way a future guest would. The story we told brought people up the mountain, and the work on the ground gave us a true story to tell. I still believe the best marketing starts close to the work.
